Days 1–3 · Watch the pros

Your day
  • 2:00 PM: Apex training. Camera on, notes open.
  • Right after: Matt's Zoom until the room closes.
  • Listen to every call. Don't multitask.
  • 8:00 PM CST: recap in Matt's Zoom.
Write down
  • The first 30 seconds of each call.
  • How objections get handled, and what comes right after.
  • Needs analysis questions.
  • How the close is framed and the app gets started.
  • Any phrase you hear twice. Steal it.

Goal by Wednesday night: walk someone through a full call, hello to signed app, without notes.

Days 4–5 · Test runs

Your day
  • 2:00 PM: Apex training.
  • Right after: Matt's Zoom for test runs.
  • Run each tool start to finish. Then again.
  • 8:00 PM CST: recap in Matt's Zoom.
Do these cold by Friday
  • Full needs analysis without prompting.
  • Navigate HP PRO while staying in conversation.
  • Start, fill and submit an EAPP end to end.
  • Explain what each step is for.

The weekend · Personal training

  • Rewrite the call flow from memory. Run needs analysis and EAPP until automatic.
  • Practice your opener out loud. Write down every question you still have and bring them Monday.

Days 6–10 · Start dialing

Your day
  • 2:00 PM: Apex training.
  • Right after: Matt's Zoom. Dial, set appointments, repeat.
  • Ask for feedback every few calls.
  • 8:00 PM CST: recap. Your numbers and what to fix.
What matters
  • Volume. Dials set appointments. Appointments close deals.
  • Consistency. Same opener, same flow.
  • Coachability. Fix it on the very next dial.
  • Tracking. Log every dial, contact and appointment.

Day 10 · Test-out

You're evaluated on
  • Your opener and control of the first minute.
  • A complete, confident needs analysis.
  • Clean HP PRO & EAPP use while talking.
  • Handling common objections.
  • Setting appointments and moving to the close.
Checklist before Friday

Pass: you're live. Dial that weekend or the following Monday. Not yet: you get specific feedback, keep working in Matt's Zoom, and test again.

Globe Life Market · AIL Division

Globe Script

01Opening

“Hi (name), my name is (your name). I work with Globe Life Insurance Company, AIL Division. I was calling you because you were online and requested a quote for life insurance. Did anybody get that out to you yet?”

See Rebuttals for how to handle responses.

“Sounds good. My job is very simple. I just need to verify the information they have for you so we can get you a proper quote and see what you can qualify for.”

“We do this over Zoom. You know what Zoom is, correct? Perfect. I'm going to send you a Zoom link. Just click on the link and let me know what it says.”

02Verify info

“I have your first name in the system as (name), and last name as (last name). Is that correct? Okay, sounds good. Please confirm your date of birth? Great. I have an address on file as (address). Is that correct?”

“And the phone number on file is the one we're speaking on, (number). Is that correct? Okay, what is the best email for you?”

“Let me ask you, what made you start looking into getting a policy?”

03Build the pain

Here's where you're going to build a lot of pain.

“Okay, and God forbid, who would be affected the most financially if you passed away tomorrow?” (Let them answer.)

“Okay, so would they be able to afford everything on their own, like the burial, casket, funeral and service, or would that be a struggle for them?”

04Health qualifications

“I'm going to ask some medical questions so we can see exactly what you may qualify for.”

  • Have you ever had any form of cancer, diabetes, heart attacks, or strokes?
  • Do you use oxygen besides the free kind outside?
  • Do you have any terminal illnesses or Alzheimer's?
  • Are you on probation, been paroled, or charged with a DUI?

→ Complete Needs Analysis

05Budget & coverage

“Now (name), I tell everyone this. When you start looking into getting a policy, there is always a number in your mind that you're thinking of when it comes to a monthly premium. What would that number be for you? What would be a comfortable monthly premium?”

“Were you looking for a specific amount of coverage? Okay, let's see what the system recommends.”

“What the system is recommending is when you pass away, your family will receive a check for $100,000. For a $100,000 policy, you will be at just $98 a month. Now (name), let me ask you this: is $98 a month comfortable for you?”

“Great, I will just need to ask a few more questions and we will get that all taken care of for you.”

06Application
  • Complete thoroughly: correct email, multiple phone numbers.
  • “Your insurance benefits are [$____] a week / [$____] per month. That amount comes out in the next two days. Every month after, it can come out between the [DAY] and [DAY]. Which day works best?”

Draft date must be within 13 days of the application date. Never the 29th, 30th, or 31st.

  • “Which bank do you use?”

Input routing and account numbers. Confirm both are correct with the Bank Verification Tool.

07Policy delivery & what to expect
  • Policies arrive within 4–8 weeks. Once received, you are permanently qualified and accepted.
  • Show the example policy envelope and your own policies: paid-up values, monthly income amounts, total face amounts. Walk through what each policy means.

When you don't yet have your own policy, show the example on HP Pro.

Service folder · set up and walk through while cementing

“This should be in your email now. Make sure you download the link to your service folder so you always have access to it.”

08Four things that can happen during qualification
1 · DeclinedEverything is sent back. I will call you and we'll work on doubling the coverage on [SPOUSE] so when something happens to one of you first, that money is there for the other.
2 · RatingThat's good news. You're approved standard for someone with your condition. I'll call and you can accept the rating and keep the benefits the same, or keep today's amount and adjust the benefit slightly.
3 · Nurse visitThe company may send a nurse at your convenience for a no-cost physical: height, weight, blood pressure. They work around your schedule.
4 · Policies arriveOnce you receive the envelope I showed you, you are covered for the rest of your life. Those are some of the most important documents your family will ever have. Keep them somewhere safe.
09Satisfaction follow-up

“Globe Life AIL Division is very service-oriented. Someone will call to confirm your satisfaction with the enrollment process. They may ask some of the same questions I did or have a few follow-up questions. That means the process is moving forward.”

10Plus lead collection

“This is very important, (name). I'm going to have you forward a text to both of your beneficiaries so they can expect my call. The reason is so they will know what to do when something happens to you.”

“Lastly (name), when you started thinking about getting a policy, did you talk to any friends or family about getting coverage in place?”

“Sounds good. We can agree that everyone should have a policy like you just got, right? Well, I can help some people you know get something set up. Let's start with who you spoke with…”

Let's say you were to go into work tomorrow and your boss says, “Work's been going really well, we're going to have you work 41 hours this week instead of 40.” With that extra hour of work, are you going to buy your dream house or car? (Pause) Of course not!

On the other hand, (name), if your boss instead says, “Work is a little slow this week, we only need you for 39 hours instead of 40,” with that difference of an hour, is that going to take food off the table for your family?

No, probably not, right? So the idea is that one hour plus or minus really isn't going to impact your family's lifestyle, but not having these concerns taken care of certainly would. So (name), in the needs analysis we went through earlier, that is why it's based on setting just one hour of your wage aside each week to protect you from life's uncertainties.
